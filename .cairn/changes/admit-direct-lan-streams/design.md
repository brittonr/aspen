# Design

The existing shell owns endpoint setup, relay-disabled direct IP paths and authenticated connections. A distinct direct-LAN profile binds peer EndpointId to peer context, service, protocol/ALPN, remote role and generation. Only the deterministic source gate can construct costly authority-handler input. Source buckets use observed IPv4 or IPv6 /64, bounded storage and injected monotonic milliseconds; transport QUIC validation is not authority admission. A trusted consumer port separately resolves current membership/principal/trust/capability evidence and opens a protected Unix socket only after exact operation preface matching. No 0-RTT path is used.

The listener's configured peer context must equal the context committed by its handoff before binding. Registered direct-stream effects require the service-bearing `Registered::proxy_stream` path: for the explicitly selected direct-stream profile, ordinary `FabricEffectPort::route` rejects `OpenStream` rather than reporting socket-transfer completion. Other profiles retain control-only stream setup for their existing bounded `SendFrame` path. The proxy binds both the registered request reference and `OpenStream.operation_id` to the protected request before applying the stream transition or dispatching protected service work.

One admitted connection and one bidirectional stream are serialized initially; no hidden transfer queue or task exists. A single driver retains partially read headers, uses two fixed frame buffers, bounded QUIC receive/send windows, one outstanding frame per direction, per-frame ACK, explicit FIN half-close, finite byte/frame work, idle and total deadlines, cancellation, reset and awaited local close. Lost ACK or partial I/O after local consumption/submission is uncertain and is never replayed. ACK means transport/socket-write observation, not durable publication or VM resume authority. Canonical Preserves/BLAKE3 aggregate evidence records actual path class, counts, duration and cleanup without exposing payloads/locators.

The operation and cleanup have separate explicit finite deadlines. DATA carries a BLAKE3 digest; ACK binds sequence and digest after protected local write. Cleanup timeout cannot become observed cleanup, even when delivery was definitively acknowledged. A handshake cancelled before a connection handle exists closes/drains its owning endpoint; unobserved cleanup retains its reservation and fences further admission rather than silently rebinding. Pending unauthenticated handshake buffers retain the selected NOQ cohort's separate explicit native bounds, not the application's frame-buffer bound.

Entering the trusted protected-service `open` future crosses a possibly effectful boundary. Cancellation, deadline expiry or an error after that entry cannot prove non-delivery, even when no socket was returned and transport byte counters are zero. The driver records entry before polling `open` and retains that conservative fact through terminal evidence; pre-open admission denial remains definite non-delivery. Dropping the public future produces no terminal receipt and does not release an unobserved listener reservation or authorize replay of a consumed registered request.

Native QUIC `ConnectionError::TimedOut` remains `Timeout` through stream acceptance, operation binding, frame/header/payload/ACK I/O and final peer-stop observation. Peer reset, early EOF and application close remain `Disconnected`. The application and native idle timers may race without changing that normalized outcome; neither deadline nor retry policy is relaxed.

Graceful completion waits for trailing EOF and acknowledged receipt of the final QUIC stream bytes before connection cleanup. The listener owns a clean connection close; the client waits for that close without racing it with a local close that could discard the listener's final ACK/FIN. Stale-generation qualification observes the listener's terminal rejection and canonical denied/no-protected-open evidence rather than requiring delivery of bytes queued before the rejected stream was closed.

Iroh 1.0.0/NOQ 1.0.0 is the selected local cohort. Kiln workflow Iroh 0.95.1 and its older separate host runtime are not silently repinned or declared interoperable. Consumer-specific operation/cutover scheduling remains Kiln-owned. Source on recovery tmpfs does not establish machine-loss durability.
